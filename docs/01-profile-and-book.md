### Aims (from the official page)

Written for business people whose main job is *not* writing code. It answers "I get that it's useful, but what exactly can it do?", "is it safe?", and "what if my company won't approve it?". Its three stated strengths: (1) you build the habit of consulting the AI about everything, (2) you can build a "semi-automation" cycle, (3) you can exploit the "compound interest of context" with a monorepo. The goal is an AI secretary that *grows the more you use it*.

### Table of contents (chapter titles, translated)

1. Why a coding agent is useful for everyday work
2. AI agent fundamentals (pricing plans and token consumption, security basics)
3. Claude Code setup and basic operation (connectors, browser extensions)
4. Build the habit of consulting the AI about everything (work folders, self-introduction by voice)
5. Practice 1: hand over the preparatory work for document creation
6. Build the "semi-automation" cycle (human → AI → human, guardrails, growing procedure files)
7. Practice 2: semi-automate expense reporting
8. Exploit the "compound interest of context" with a monorepo
9. Practice 3: accelerate project work with compound interest
10. Expand the grown workplace in four directions (spec-driven development, removing the "AI smell", parallel sessions, phone instructions, team assets, reuse with other agents)
11. Overcoming the corporate "security wall"

### Review roundup (note)

The author collected 11 review blog posts: https://note.com/minorun365/n/n097d72bd262b (Sep 25, 2026; updated Oct 31, 2026), which also links to a book support page tracking post-publication spec changes and pitfalls.

Recurring praise points across reviewers:

- **"Semi-automation"** — not full automation; the human approves at key points (most-mentioned idea)
- The "monorepo" practice of gathering all daily work into one repository
- Transferability: Codex / Kiro / Copilot users found it applicable, not Claude-Code-only
- The framing of an AI secretary you "raise" at your side
- A table of contents organized by *work you want to do*, not by tool features

Note: this is the author's own roundup of favorable reviews; it contains no critical takes.
